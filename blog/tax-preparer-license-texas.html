Do Tax Preparers Need a License in Texas?

2026-10-02

Short answer: no, Texas does not require tax preparers to hold a state-issued license. Unlike a handful of other states, Texas leaves tax preparation largely unregulated at the state level. That surprises a lot of people, especially after trusting someone with sensitive financial information.

So what keeps preparers accountable?

Even without a Texas license, federal rules still apply. Anyone who prepares federal tax returns for pay is generally required to have a Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN) issued by the IRS. That number has to appear on every return they sign. It's a baseline, not a guarantee of quality, but it's a starting point for accountability.

Beyond the PTIN, some preparers carry additional credentials — things like CPA, Enrolled Agent, or attorney status — that come with their own licensing boards, continuing education requirements, and ethics rules. Those credentials matter most if your situation is complex or if you ever need someone to represent you in front of the IRS.

Why this matters when you're comparing preparers

Because Texas doesn't filter out unqualified preparers for you, the responsibility shifts to you as the client. A friendly office and a low price don't tell you much about whether someone understands your specific situation — self-employment income, rental property, a small business, or a notice from the IRS.

This is part of why fees vary so much from one preparer to the next, and why "cheapest" isn't always the right question to ask.

What to ask before you hire someone

  • Do you have a PTIN, and will it appear on my return?
  • What's your background — bookkeeping, payroll, tax planning, or representation work?
  • Can you help if the IRS ever sends a letter about my return?
  • Do you work with businesses like mine, or mostly individual returns?

A preparer who can answer these clearly, without dodging, is usually a safer bet than one who can't.
