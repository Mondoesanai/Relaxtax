How to Actually Reach a Person at the IRS

2026-10-31

If you've ever tried calling the IRS, you already know the drill: long menus, long holds, and sometimes a dropped call before you ever reach a human. There's no secret trick that guarantees a quick pickup, but a few habits can improve your odds — and in some cases, it's smarter to skip the hold music altogether.

Call Early and Call Early in the Week

Phone lines tend to be busiest later in the day and around deadlines. If you have a choice, try calling as close to when the lines open as possible, and earlier in the week rather than Friday afternoon. Mondays right after a holiday or filing deadline are usually the worst time to try.

Have Your Information Ready Before You Dial

Before you call, gather the documents you'll likely be asked about: your Social Security number or EIN, the tax year in question, any notice or letter you received, and your most recent filed return. Having this in front of you means you won't have to call back a second time because you couldn't answer a question.

Listen Carefully to the Menu Options

Automated systems route calls based on your selections, so pick the option that most closely matches your actual issue — a general "tax question" option may route you somewhere that can't help with a specific notice. If you received a letter from the IRS, it often has a specific phone number and extension printed on it that connects you faster than the general line.

When It Makes Sense to Let Someone Else Make the Call

Not every issue needs you to sit on hold yourself. If you're dealing with an audit, a collections notice, or a dispute that's dragged on, it can be worth having someone represent you with the IRS directly. Not everyone is allowed to do this — it generally takes a credential like an Enrolled Agent, CPA, or attorney.
