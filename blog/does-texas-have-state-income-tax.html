Does Texas Have State Income Tax?

2026-11-21

No — Texas is one of the states with no state income tax. That means your paycheck isn't reduced by a state withholding line, and you don't file a Texas return reporting your wages, interest, or business income. But "no state income tax" doesn't mean "no taxes" or "no paperwork," and it's worth understanding what it actually changes for you.

What "no state income tax" really means

Texas doesn't tax wages, salaries, retirement income, or most other personal income. If you're used to filing a state return in another state, moving to or living in Texas simplifies that part of your life — there's no separate state Form 1040 equivalent to prepare each spring.

You still file a federal return with the IRS every year. Federal income tax rules don't change based on which state you live in.

What Texas taxes instead

Texas makes up for the lack of income tax in other ways — most notably sales tax and property tax. If you own a home or business property in Collin County, your property tax bill is often a bigger line item than it would be in a state with income tax. Business owners may also deal with the Texas franchise tax, depending on how their entity is structured.

None of this shows up on your federal return, but it's part of the bigger financial picture — especially for business owners trying to plan cash flow across the year.

Where this can get confusing

A few situations trip people up:

  • You moved from another state mid-year. You may still owe that state's income tax for the months you lived there, even after you've relocated to Texas.
  • You work remotely for an out-of-state employer. Depending on the employer's payroll setup, withholding rules can get messy.
  • You run a business. No state income tax doesn't mean no state filings at all — franchise tax and sales tax obligations still apply in many cases.

These are exactly the kinds of details that are easy to miss if you're filing on your own, especially the first year after a move or a business change.

What this means for your federal return

Since Texas doesn't collect income tax, your federal return is really the whole story for most individuals. That makes it worth getting right — correct withholding, accurate deductions, and proper reporting of any out-of-state income you may still owe elsewhere.

If you're self-employed or run a small business in Plano, Allen, Frisco, or McKinney, good bookkeeping throughout the year makes that federal filing much less stressful when it's due.
