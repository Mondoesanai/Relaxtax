Who Can Help Me Pay My Back Taxes?

2026-11-14

If you owe back taxes, the honest answer is that several different people can help — but they help in different ways. The right choice depends on how complicated your situation is and whether the IRS has already started contacting you.

Start with someone who can talk to the IRS on your behalf

Not everyone is legally allowed to represent you in front of the IRS. Enrolled agents, CPAs, and tax attorneys generally can. A bookkeeper or an unlicensed preparer may be great at other things, but representation during an audit or collections matter is a different skill set entirely.

If you're not sure who's qualified to step in, it's worth asking directly before you share sensitive financial details with anyone.

Understand what "help" actually looks like

Dealing with back taxes usually involves one or more of these steps:

  • Figuring out exactly what you owe, including any missing or unfiled returns
  • Reviewing your options for resolving the balance — this can include setting up a payment plan with the IRS
  • Communicating with the IRS directly so you're not doing it alone
  • Making a plan going forward so the same problem doesn't repeat next year

A good tax professional should be able to walk you through which of these steps apply to you, in plain language — not just hand you paperwork.

Don't wait for it to feel "small enough" to deal with

Back tax situations tend to get more complicated the longer they sit, not less. Letters pile up, deadlines pass, and what started as one unfiled return can turn into several. The earlier you loop in someone who knows the process, the more options are usually still on the table.

Where this fits with bookkeeping and tax planning

Back taxes rarely happen in isolation. Often they're tied to messy bookkeeping, a missed filing, or a business that grew faster than its paperwork. That's why resolving a back tax issue often works best alongside a broader look at your bookkeeping and ongoing tax planning — not just a one-time fix.
