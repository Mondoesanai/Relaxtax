Is an Enrolled Agent Like a CPA?

2026-11-07

Short answer: they're similar in that both are trusted tax professionals, but they're not the same credential. An enrolled agent (EA) is licensed by the federal government specifically to handle tax matters. A CPA (Certified Public Accountant) is licensed by a state board and covers a much broader range of accounting work, of which tax is only one part.

What makes an enrolled agent different

Enrolled agents earn their credential directly through the IRS rather than through a state. Their focus is taxes — preparation, planning, and representing clients in front of the IRS. Because the EA credential is federal, an enrolled agent can generally represent a client anywhere in the country, not just in the state where they're located.

What makes a CPA different

CPAs are licensed state by state and their training covers a wide range of accounting topics — audits, financial statements, corporate accounting, and more, in addition to taxes. A CPA may specialize heavily in tax work, or they may focus on other areas of accounting entirely. The CPA license itself doesn't tell you how much of their practice is tax-focused.

Why the distinction matters for you

If your main need is tax preparation, tax planning, or help responding to an IRS notice, both an EA and a tax-focused CPA can be good fits — what matters most is their actual experience with situations like yours. If you need broader accounting services like audited financial statements, you'll specifically need a CPA, since that work falls outside what an EA is licensed to do.

Either way, it's worth asking directly: how much of your work is tax-related, and have you handled situations similar to mine?

What to ask before you choose

  • Are you an enrolled agent, a CPA, or both?
  • How much of your practice is focused on tax work specifically?
  • Can you represent me directly if the IRS sends a letter or starts an audit?
  • Do you also offer bookkeeping or payroll, in case my needs grow?
